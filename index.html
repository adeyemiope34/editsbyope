What I do

Three edits. One question first: which one do you need.

Most first drafts need developmental editing before anything else. I'll read your manuscript and tell you where it stands.

I.

Developmental editing

Works on structure: what order the chapters go in, what's missing, where the reader loses patience, and whether the book delivers what the opening promises.

For a first draft that isn't finding its shape yet.

II.

Line editing

For manuscripts where the thinking is already sound but the sentences are heavy. Smooths the prose toward what you were trying to say — never toward how I'd have said it.

For a solid draft that still reads like a draft.

III.

Copy editing

Line-by-line grammar, consistency, and style, edited to the Chicago Manual of Style, for a manuscript that is nearly ready to publish.

For a finished manuscript before it goes to KDP.

Fixed price, agreed upfront: $0.02–$0.06 per word depending on the stage and complexity of the edit. Also available: ebook formatting and cover design for KDP, on request.

Questions clients ask

Frequently asked questions

What kinds of books do you edit?

Christian and faith-based nonfiction: devotionals, Bible studies and teaching books, testimony and ministry memoir, discipleship material, and Christian leadership books.

Which kind of editing do I need?

Developmental editing works on structure — what order the chapters go in, what's missing, where the reader loses patience, whether the book delivers what the opening promises. Line editing works on how it reads when the thinking is already sound but the sentences are heavy. Copy editing works line by line on grammar, consistency, and style to the Chicago Manual of Style, for a book that's nearly final. Most first drafts need developmental first. Send the manuscript and I'll tell you which one you need, including when the answer is the cheaper one.

Will you change my theology?

No. I edit how clearly your message is carried, not what you believe. Scripture references, doctrinal positions, and denominational language stay yours. Where a passage could be misread by someone outside your tradition, I flag it in a comment and the decision stays with you.

Will you change my voice?

No. I edit toward what you were trying to say, not toward how I would have said it. If a change alters your meaning, I raise it as a query rather than making it silently.

What do I actually receive?

A Word file with tracked changes, a clean copy, and a short editorial summary of the patterns I found and what to watch for in your next book. Margin notes are written in plain English, not editorial shorthand.

How long does it take?

A 50,000-word copy edit runs one to two weeks. Developmental work at that length runs longer — I'll confirm the exact date and duration before the contract starts.

What does it cost?

Fixed price, agreed before we begin. I charge $0.02–$0.06 per word, depending on the stage of the edit your manuscript needs and the complexity of the edit.

What do I need to have ready?

A complete draft. Not a perfect one — a complete one.

Can I see your work before hiring?

Yes. I edit the first 1,000 words of your manuscript for free, so you're judging an actual edit rather than a promise.

Do you work with self-published (KDP) authors?

Yes — most of the manuscripts I edit are headed for Amazon KDP. I can also point you to formatting and cover design once the edit is done.

What if my manuscript needs more than one type of edit?

Common, especially on a first book. I'll usually recommend doing developmental, then line, then copy editing in that order, and price each stage separately so you can pause between them.

How do we get started?

Message me on WhatsApp or by email with your genre, word count, and the stage your draft is at. I'll tell you which edit it needs and what it will cost before you spend anything.
